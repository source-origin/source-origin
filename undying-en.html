Ecosystem Preface

Rewriting two decades of open-source rules

For twenty years, open-source has been an unspoken form of exploitation. Countless developers contribute code, architectures, AI-agents, documentation and technical breakthroughs, forming the foundation of the global internet and AI industry. Nevertheless, commercial gains, platform valuations and capital dividends are overwhelmingly captured by centralized platforms and investors.

Developers do most of the work and receive the least reward. Contributions lack formal on-chain attestation, verifiable credentials and enforced distribution mechanisms. Revenue sharing depends entirely on personal relationships, licenses and moral restraint. Records can be altered, contributions erased, and rights left unprotected.

Around the world, many teams are waking up and rebuilding open-source production relationships: implementing repository mirroring, contribution metrics, value tagging and contribution accounting.

Yet nearly all face one critical limitation: They can calculate your value, but they cannot lock in your rewards. All metrics, weights and allocations run inside centralized databases, subject to modification, reset or reversal at any time.
